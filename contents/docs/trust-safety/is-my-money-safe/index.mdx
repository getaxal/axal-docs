The short answer: Axal never holds your funds, your keys are protected by hardware-level security,
and Axal's systems are independently audited — but Axal is not a bank, and your balance is not
FDIC- or SIPC-insured.

## You hold your own wallet

Axal is non-custodial. Every account gets its own self-custodial wallet, and only you control its
private key — not Axal, not Privy (the wallet infrastructure provider). You can export your wallet
or withdraw at any time. See [How your wallet works](/docs/trust-safety/how-your-wallet-works).

## Your keys are protected by hardware-level security

Axal signs transactions inside a Trusted Execution Environment (the same class of technology
behind Face ID) — an isolated environment nobody at Axal, including Axal staff, can access or
tamper with, even for debugging. See [How your wallet works](/docs/trust-safety/how-your-wallet-works#trusted-execution-environments-tees)
for the technical detail, including a real cryptographic attestation.

## Axal is independently audited

Axal's systems are audited by [Sherlock](https://www.sherlock.xyz/), a third-party web3 security
firm, and the signer code is open source for anyone to review. Axal also runs a live bug bounty.
See [Audits](/docs/trust-safety/audits).

## What's not covered

Axal is not a bank and your balance is not insured the way a bank deposit is. Yield comes from
real lending markets, which carry smart-contract, market, and counterparty risk. Read
[Risks](/docs/trust-safety/risks) and [Axal vs a traditional savings account](/docs/how-it-works/axal-vs-savings)
before deciding how much to hold in Axal.
