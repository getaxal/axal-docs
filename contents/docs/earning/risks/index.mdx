Axal earns yield by lending your dollars through overcollateralized stablecoin markets. That is a different risk profile from a bank savings account. This page explains what can go wrong, what Axal does about it, and how that differs from conventional products. For the full product walkthrough, see [How Axal Works](/docs/learn/how-axal-works). For a shorter safety summary, see [Is Axal Safe?](/docs/learn/is-axal-safe).

## How yield is generated (and why that matters for risk)

When you deposit dollars, Axal converts them to USDC (digital dollars issued by Circle) and lends them in established overcollateralized lending markets such as Morpho and Euler. Borrowers must post more collateral than they borrow. If they fail to repay or their collateral falls below safe levels, that collateral is liquidated and used to protect lenders.

Your principal is designed to be protected by those collateral rules — not by FDIC insurance, and not by Axal holding your money. Axal is self-custodial: you control the keys to your funds.

## Key risks

### Software / smart-contract risk

A bug or exploit in a lending market Axal uses could cause losses. A failure in liquidation logic could also leave lenders exposed if borrowers stop repaying or do not top up collateral in time.

**What Axal does:** Only allocate to blue-chip markets with significant liquidity, multiple independent audits, and meaningful institutional usage. Diversify across markets so a problem in one does not take down the whole allocation. Continuously monitor rates, liquidity, and other risk signals, and reallocate when something looks unsafe.

### Dollar-peg risk

USDC is designed to stay at $1, but in a crisis it can temporarily trade below that. Because Axal lends dollars only via USDC, a de-peg would affect balances denominated in USDC.

**What Axal does:** Stick to USDC issued by Circle, a publicly traded company, rather than less-proven “dollar yield” products that may not actually hold dollars under the hood. Avoid mixing in other asset risk for the cash product.

### Market stress and liquidity risk

In extreme conditions, withdrawals can be slower, markets can become thin, or collateral prices can move faster than liquidations can keep up. Rates are also dynamic: supply and demand change the APY you see over time.

**What Axal does:** Prefer deep, liquid markets so withdrawals can stay available. Use conservative collateral and LTV standards so liquidations have room to work during sharp moves. Run a rebalancer that moves funds based on rates, liquidity, and risk — with scoped permissions on what it can and cannot do.

### No FDIC / not a bank

Axal is a financial technology company, not a bank. Balances are not FDIC insured. Traditional bank “runs” and deposit freezes do not apply in the same way, because you always control your wallet and can move funds without Axal’s permission — but that also means there is no government deposit guarantee.

**What Axal does:** Keep the product self-custodial by design (via Privy). No lockups or minimums: deposit or withdraw when you want, including to external bank accounts. For a plain-language comparison, see [Is Axal Safe?](/docs/learn/is-axal-safe).

### Regulatory risk

Rules around stablecoins, DeFi, and consumer yield products can change. New requirements could affect how the product works, who can use it, or how it is taxed.

**What Axal does:** Follow applicable law, including geofencing where required, and monitor regulatory developments. USDC itself sits under a clearer U.S. federal framework than many earlier crypto cash products.

## What Axal is not exposed to (for cash yield)

Relative to older multi-tier or leveraged strategy designs, Axal’s cash product is intentionally narrower:

- **No leveraged “aggressive” strategy tiers** for users to pick between.
- **No lending of non-dollar assets** for the cash balance — dollars only, via USDC.
- **No custodial hold** where Axal going offline locks your money; you retain control of the keys.

Borrowers in the underlying markets may use volatile collateral (for example Bitcoin), but those loans are overcollateralized and liquidated by market rules when ratios break. That borrower-side volatility is part of why lender protection is designed the way it is — see the example in [How Axal Works](/docs/learn/how-axal-works).

## How you can manage risk personally

- Only deposit money you can afford to put at risk for a higher, variable yield.
- Understand that APY moves with the market; past rates do not guarantee future returns.
- Prefer starting small if you are new, then scale as you get comfortable with deposits and withdrawals.
- Keep account recovery options up to date so you do not lose access to a self-custodial wallet.
- Read the formal [risk disclosures](/docs/legal/risk-disclosures) before using Axal.
