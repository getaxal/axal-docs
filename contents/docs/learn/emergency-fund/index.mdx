*By Ash Ahmed, co-founder of Axal. Updated October 2026.*

Keep your emergency fund somewhere safe and instantly reachable, like an FDIC-insured high-yield savings account. Put savings beyond that where they can earn more. Splitting your cash into tiers lets you do both instead of choosing between safety and yield.

## Step 1: Size your emergency fund

A common rule of thumb is three to six months of essential expenses: rent or mortgage, utilities, groceries, insurance, minimum debt payments. Lean toward six months or more if your income is irregular or you're the only earner in your household.

## Step 2: Put it somewhere safe and liquid

Your emergency fund's job is to be there the day you need it, at full value. Good homes:

- **High-yield savings account:** FDIC insured, top accounts pay about 4.2–4.5% APY (October 2026), withdraw anytime.
- **Money market account:** FDIC insured, often with checks or a debit card for fast access.

Even here, don't settle for 0.5% at a big bank. Moving your emergency fund to a top HYSA can earn many times more with the same protection.

## Step 3: Tier everything else

| Tier | What it's for | Where it can go |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Emergency fund | Job loss, medical bills, urgent repairs | High-yield savings or money market account |
| 2. Known expenses in the next 1–2 years | A down payment, tuition, a car | CDs or Treasury bills matched to the date |
| 3. Extra savings | Money you won't need soon but aren't investing | Higher-yield options, if you accept their risks |
