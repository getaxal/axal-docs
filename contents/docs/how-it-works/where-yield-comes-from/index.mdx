Axal doesn't generate yield itself — it routes your idle balance into established lending markets
and automatically moves it to wherever the safe, available return is best. This page explains that
in plain terms, without assuming you know crypto.

## The short version

1. You deposit USD or Bitcoin.
2. When you tap **Start earning**, Axal deploys that balance into vetted lending protocols —
   places other people borrow from, paying interest, similar in spirit to how a bank lends out
   deposits.
3. That interest accrues to your balance continuously. Axal monitors these positions and can move
   your funds or exit entirely if something looks unsafe.
4. You can move funds back to idle, or withdraw, at any time.

## Who's actually paying the yield?

The yield comes from borrowers, not from Axal or from other users' deposits. On the lending
protocols Axal uses (for example Morpho and Aave), borrowers post collateral and pay interest to
borrow against it. That interest is what generates the APY you see in the app. Axal doesn't set
this rate — it reflects real borrowing demand and can move up or down.

## Why route across protocols instead of picking one?

Rates on any single lending market change as more or less money flows in and out of it. By
continuously comparing rates across multiple vetted protocols and networks, Axal can move your
balance to wherever the safe rate is currently best, rather than leaving it parked in one place
that's gone stale. This is done automatically — you don't have to compare rates or move funds
yourself.

## What Axal checks before using a protocol

Not every lending market is included. Axal limits itself to protocols that meet a bar for safety
history and liquidity, and the strategy stack is intentionally narrow rather than chasing every
available yield source — see [Strategy stack](/docs/how-it-works/strategy-stack) for what's
currently in use.

## Key terms

- **APY (Annual Percentage Yield)** — the rate your balance earns over a year, including
  compounding. It moves with market conditions and is not fixed.
- **Idle balance** — funds sitting in your account that are not currently earning yield.
- **Yield-earning balance** — funds actively deployed into a strategy and accruing interest.
- **Lending protocol** — a piece of software where lenders and borrowers meet on-chain; interest
  paid by borrowers is what funds the yield.
- **Non-custodial** — Axal never takes ownership of your funds; you hold your own wallet and can
  withdraw or export it at any time.
- **Smart wallet** — the self-custodial wallet automatically created for you when you sign up.
- **Rebalancing** — Axal automatically moving your balance between protocols to keep it earning
  the best available safe rate.
- **De-peg** — when a stablecoin's market price drops below its intended $1 value; see
  [Risks](/docs/trust-safety/risks).
- **Liquidation** — the forced closing of a leveraged position when its collateral value falls too
  far; not something that happens to a standard Axal USD or BTC deposit, but relevant background
  if you read about DeFi lending elsewhere.
